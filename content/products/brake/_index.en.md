Air brake failures share a pattern: they **degrade gradually rather than failing outright**, so drivers adapt to a sick system and only discover it at inspection or in an emergency.

## Valves are the core

The **four-circuit protection valve** splits the air system into four independent circuits so a leak in one does not disable the other three. When it fails, one circuit builds pressure slowly — or has none at all.

The **air dryer** removes water and oil. Once the desiccant is spent, moisture enters the lines: frozen valves in winter, corroded spools in the tropics. It is the most overlooked and cheapest part to replace.

The **hand brake valve** and **brake master valve** are the control end; leakage shows up as pressure slowly bleeding down after parking.

## Chassis parts

Thrust rods and stabiliser bars hold axle geometry; shock absorbers control oscillation. Wear in these three causes irregular tyre wear — and customers usually replace tyres first and check the chassis second, which is the wrong order.

## Ordering advice

Air components differ by **port thread size**, which can vary between model years of the same truck. Give us the VIN and we will identify the exact valve body.

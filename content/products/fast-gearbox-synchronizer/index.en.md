Fast transmissions are fitted to a very large share of Chinese heavy trucks, which makes synchronizers a consumable. Diagnosing correctly before ordering saves real money.

## Three symptoms, three different causes

**Gear clash, especially on downshifts** — worn synchronizer ring cones; friction drops and the ring can no longer equalise shaft speeds in time. Most common case, and rings alone fix it.

**Popping out of gear while driving** — usually not the synchronizer at all, but worn **sliding sleeve teeth or hub splines**, a bent shift fork, or tired detent springs. New rings will not cure this.

**One gear hard to select, others fine** — check that gear's fork and rail first, then the synchronizer.

## Supply options

- **Rings only** — the economical repair when cones are worn but teeth are intact
- **Complete synchronizer assembly** — ring, sleeve and hub together, for worn teeth
- **Small repair kit** — circlips, shims, detent components

## The point most people miss

On the 12JS and 16JS twin-countershaft boxes, main-box and range-box synchronizers wear at different rates. Replacing only the main box while the range box rings are already worn leaves the customer convinced the repair failed. Check both during teardown.

Steering parts fall into two groups: those affecting **feel** (column, telescopic shaft, wheel) and those affecting **wheel geometry** (steering gear, tie rods).

## Steering gear

SHACMAN mainly uses recirculating-ball power steering gears. Warning signs are **increased free play**, slower self-centring, or a notchy feel when turning. Waiting until it leaks badly usually means the Pitman shaft is already worn.

## Column and telescopic shaft

Cab tilt and perished cab mounts change the relative position of column and shaft, showing up as wheel shake or noise. Once the shaft splines wear, steering develops a distinct dead zone.

## An easy part to confuse

The **steering oil tank filter** is frequently ordered under the wrong number, mistaken for a hydraulic oil filter. In the catalogue it sits under steering, not engine lubrication — worth checking when ordering.

## Safety note

Steering components are a safety-inspection focus. For markets with mandatory inspection (some Middle East countries) genuine-part documentation is expected with the shipment. We provide SHACMAN part number cross-references, not fabricated certificates.

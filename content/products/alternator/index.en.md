Alternator failures on heavy trucks usually present as "the batteries keep going flat", but the real cause is often elsewhere.

## Start with a voltage check

Measure battery terminal voltage at idle after start-up:

- **27.5–28.5 V** — charging correctly
- **Below 27 V** — undercharging; check belt tension and earth straps first
- **Above 29 V** — regulator failed; it will boil the batteries, replace it
- **Fine at idle but drops to 26 V with headlights on** — undersized unit or a failed rectifier diode

## Sizing

80 A is the baseline for standard cargo trucks. Fleets running **parking coolers, reefer units or long night shifts** should specify 100 A or 120 A. An undersized alternator runs at full load continuously and its life drops sharply.

## Supply

Built-in regulator is standard; external-regulator variants also available. Single and multi-groove pulleys — state the original pulley type when ordering.

Full-load and insulation tests at the factory. **Warranty 6 months**, because alternator life is heavily influenced by vehicle wiring — poor earthing and tired batteries will shorten the life of even a good unit.

Clutch life depends heavily on duty cycle: long-haul linehaul units often exceed 400,000 km, while city tipper trucks can wear down to the rivets in 150,000 km.

## Assembly or disc only

Replacing just the disc is the cheap route — but only valid if the **pressure plate diaphragm spring has not been annealed**. If the cover face is blued, shows heat cracks, or the diaphragm fingers are dished, fitting only a disc gives you a heavy pedal, a high bite point and a repeat teardown in three months.

Check cover flatness with a straight edge. Beyond 0.3 mm or with visible hot spots, replace the assembly.

## Do not skip the release bearing

It is the cheapest part in the assembly and the most expensive to replace — it costs a full re-and-re. Always renew the release bearing and guide sleeve with the clutch.

## Before you bolt it up

- Flywheel face: heat cracks, flatness within tolerance
- Pilot bearing in the crank tail: spins freely
- Release fork and levers: wear within limits

Fitting a new clutch onto a scored flywheel simply burns the new disc through uneven contact.

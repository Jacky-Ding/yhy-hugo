Brake chamber failure is usually gradual, not sudden — braking force slowly weakens, the driver compensates with a longer pedal, and the problem only surfaces at inspection or in an emergency.

## Typical failure sequence

1. **Diaphragm ageing and cracking** — rubber hardens under UV, ozone and oil, cracking from the edge inward
2. **Spring fatigue** — slow return, delayed release, dragging brakes and hot drums
3. **Pushrod seal leakage** — pressure bleeds down overnight, soft first pedal in the morning
4. **Housing corrosion perforation** — common in humid coastal regions

## Sizing

T24 and T30 differ in effective area, which directly sets braking force. **Both chambers on an axle must be the same size** — mixing them causes pull to one side, which is indefensible at inspection or after an accident.

Dual-chamber units (T24/30) serve the spring parking brake. When replacing, confirm whether the service or parking chamber failed; replacing the complete unit is usually simpler.

## Testing

Every chamber is pressure-held at 0.8 MPa for leak testing, and diaphragms are cold-fold tested at −40 °C. A second visual and port check is done before container loading.

The crankshaft is one of the most expensive single components in the engine, and deciding whether to grind or replace is where experience pays off.

## Grindable vs. scrap

If journal **ovality and taper** stay within 0.05 mm, the shaft can usually be ground to the next undersize and matched with thicker bearings. Replace it instead when you find any of these:

- **Transverse cracks** on the journal surface (longitudinal hairline marks are usually acceptable)
- **Coaxiality out of tolerance** between rod and main journals that grinding cannot restore
- Evidence of a **spun bearing seizure** — blued, annealed journals
- **Bending** beyond 0.15 mm

## Sizing

The WD615 uses a 0.25 mm step. We stock standard plus 0.25 and 0.50 undersize, so you order against the actual material to be removed rather than buying standard and discovering it will not fit.

## Do not forget the bearings

Always fit new bearings with a new or re-ground shaft, and match the bearing grade to the shaft size exactly. Check thrust washer clearance at the same time — excessive end play is usually worn thrust washers, not a bad crankshaft.
